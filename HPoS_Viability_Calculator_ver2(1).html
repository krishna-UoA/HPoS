Where each option's costs come from

Under the subscription the fee is split into what the provider spends it on: maintenance, insurance, overheads and a risk allowance, the replacement reserve, loan principal and interest, tax, and its equity return (net of the residual value it keeps). Electricity and any hybrid gas are paid by the owner unless electricity is included in the fee. The categories add up exactly to each option's total.

Scenarios

How the monthly fee is worked out

The calculated fee is the lowest Year-1 fee, rising each year with indexation, that lets the provider earn its target return on equity after tax while every year's debt service cover stays above the lender's minimum. Debt is sized at the lower of the gearing cap and what that cover test allows.

Testing a different fee

Move the fee marker to see what happens at another fee. Below the calculated fee the provider earns less than its target and the lender will lend less. Above the owner's break-even, buying the heat pump outright costs the owner less. When no fee suits both sides, the calculator estimates the grant, or the lower provider return, that would close the gap.

What the fee covers

Recovery of the investment and a return on it, loan repayments and interest, maintenance, insurance, the monitoring platform, provider overheads, a reserve for major replacements and an allowance for availability penalties and bad debt. Electricity is paid by the owner unless “Electricity included in the fee” is switched on, in which case the provider buys it and adds a risk premium.

The three options

  • Keep gas: gas bills including the EU ETS2 carbon cost from 2028, boiler maintenance and a boiler replacement when due.
  • Buy the heat pump: the same system financed with a loan; electricity with a performance gap for self-managed systems, the owner's own maintenance and replacements. The owner keeps the residual value.
  • HPoS subscription: the fee, plus the heat pump's electricity if it is not included, plus any gas still used by a hybrid system.

Costs are compared as net present values over the contract term using the owner's cost of capital, converted from real to nominal terms with the inflation assumption.

Where the costs come from

The cost breakdown splits all three options into the same categories, so the difference between buying and subscribing can be traced line by line. The subscription fee is split using the provider's own cash flows: maintenance, insurance, overheads, the replacement reserve, what it pays the lender, its tax and what is left for its shareholders. The categories add up exactly to each option's total.

Sizing the heat pump and enabling works

New projects can estimate the heat pump capacity from the heat demand: annual heat ÷ typical full-load hours for the building type, adjusted for a cold, temperate or warm climate. A hybrid is sized on a simplified load-duration curve, so a heat pump supplying 70% of the heat needs about 45% of the peak load. Enabling works can be set as a rule-of-thumb share of the heat pump cost (12–25% depending on building type). Both are screening estimates: replace them with a heat-loss survey, a sizing tool or contractor quotes when available.
